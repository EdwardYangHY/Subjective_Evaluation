Instructions and scoring rubric

Task: Judge how faithfully the target-language translation conveys the meaning of the source transcript. Assess meaning rather than stylistic preference. Do not penalize natural paraphrases, word order changes, or differences in tone unless the meaning changes. If needed, consult the provided local conversation context for referents and ambiguity.

ScoreLabelDefinition
5Fully accurateAll important source meaning is preserved; no substantive semantic errors.
4Mostly accurateMain meaning is preserved; only minor omissions/imprecision that do not materially affect interpretation.
3Partially accurateMain point is recognizable, but notable information is omitted, distorted, or mistranslated.
2Substantially inaccurateImportant parts of the meaning are wrong or missing.
1IncorrectTranslation is largely unrelated to or contradicts the source.

Major semantic error (Yes/No): A material mistake that changes or omits important meaning, e.g. wrong negation, participants, key facts, quantities, actions, or hallucinated content. Ordinary synonyms, grammatical variation, or acceptable paraphrases are not major errors. Major-error judgment and adequacy are distinct fields; do not programmatically infer one from the other.

Languages may alternate between items. Judge independently of other annotators. Do not use external LLMs or translation software while rating. If uncertain, use the comment box. Context is for interpretation and is not content to score.
